Vietnam eVisa for Sri Lanka, Israel, Mauritius, Egypt & Other Nationalities

Some eVisa applications need more care. We help you prepare yours properly and follow it through.

Citizens of every country and territory can apply for a Vietnam eVisa. But in practice, applications from some nationalities are reviewed more carefully. They can take longer, receive requests for additional information, or be refused if details are incomplete or unclear.

If you hold a passport from Sri Lanka, Israel, Mauritius, Egypt or another country where eVisa applications often need extra attention, a well-prepared application makes a real difference. Oravia has hands-on experience with these cases and can guide you from the first step.

Why some applications need extra care

  • Applications may go through additional checks before a decision is made
  • The purpose of travel and the itinerary need to be clear and consistent
  • Small inconsistencies, such as names, dates or addresses that do not match, are more likely to cause delays
  • Processing can take longer than the standard timeframe, so applying late is riskier

Nationalities we help with

Vietnam eVisa for Sri Lankan Citizens

Sri Lankan passport holders need a visa to visit Vietnam and can apply for an eVisa. We help Sri Lankan travellers prepare a clear, complete application for tourism, family visits or business trips, and follow it until the result is ready.

Vietnam eVisa for Israeli Citizens

Israeli passport holders need a visa to visit Vietnam and can apply for an eVisa. We help you get the details right, choose single or multiple entry for your itinerary, and apply early enough before your trip.

Vietnam eVisa for Mauritian Citizens

Mauritian passport holders need a visa to visit Vietnam and can apply for an eVisa. We guide you through every field of the application and check your documents, so your trip to Vietnam starts without surprises.

Vietnam eVisa for Egyptian Citizens

Egyptian passport holders need a visa to visit Vietnam and can apply for an eVisa. We help you present a clear purpose of travel and a consistent application, and we stay in touch until your eVisa is issued.

Other nationalities

We also help travellers from other countries whose eVisa applications may need additional review. Not sure whether this applies to you? Send us your nationality and travel dates and we will tell you.

What to prepare

  • Passport valid for at least six months from your entry date
  • A clear image of your passport data page and a recent portrait photo
  • Your planned entry and exit dates and entry airport or border
  • Flight details and your first address in Vietnam, such as your hotel booking
  • For business travel: the name and contact details of the company you will visit in Vietnam

Our advice

  • Apply at least two to three weeks before your trip
  • Make sure every detail matches your passport exactly
  • Do not submit several applications for the same trip
  • Book flights you can change until your eVisa is approved, where possible

Frequently Asked Questions

Can citizens of Sri Lanka, Israel, Mauritius and Egypt apply for a Vietnam eVisa?

Yes. Citizens of all countries and territories can apply for a Vietnam eVisa. Applications from some nationalities may take longer or need additional information, so careful preparation is important.

Why is my eVisa application taking longer than expected?

Some applications go through additional checks, especially when information is unclear or incomplete. Send us your application code and we will check your case.

Can Oravia guarantee my eVisa approval?

No. Visa decisions are made by the Vietnamese immigration authorities. We help you submit a complete, accurate and consistent application, which reduces avoidable problems.

My eVisa was refused. What can I do?

It is important to understand what may have caused the refusal before applying again. See our Fix eVisa service or contact us with your application code.

How early should I apply?

We recommend applying at least two to three weeks before your trip, and earlier around public holidays such as Tet.
