Vietnam eVisa for Chinese Citizens

From Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou or Kunming to Vietnam, with your eVisa prepared correctly.

Vietnam is one of the most popular destinations for travellers from China, whether for Ha Long Bay, Da Nang, Nha Trang, Phu Quoc or a business trip to Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. Citizens of the People's Republic of China holding ordinary passports need a visa to enter Vietnam, and the Vietnam eVisa is the easiest way to get one.

Vietnam eVisa for Chinese citizens at a glance

  • Stay: up to 90 days
  • Entry: single or multiple entry
  • Apply: online, with the result delivered electronically
  • Enter Vietnam: by air through the international airports, or overland through designated Vietnam–China border gates such as Huu Nghi (Lang Son), Lao Cai and Mong Cai

Common mistakes Chinese travellers make

  • Name order: enter your surname and given names in pinyin exactly as printed in your passport. Do not use Chinese characters.
  • Passport details: passport number, date of birth and expiry date must match your passport exactly
  • Wrong entry point: if you plan to cross by land, choose the correct border gate, not an airport
  • Single vs multiple entry: choose multiple entry if you will leave and return to Vietnam, for example on a cross-border trip
  • Photo: use a recent, clear photo with a plain background and no glasses

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Chinese citizens need a visa for Vietnam?

Yes. Citizens of the People's Republic of China holding ordinary passports need a visa to enter Vietnam. The eVisa is the most convenient option for most trips.

Can Chinese citizens use the Vietnam eVisa at land borders?

Yes. The eVisa can be used at designated land border gates, including several on the Vietnam–China border such as Huu Nghi, Lao Cai and Mong Cai. Check that your entry point is on the official list before you travel.

How long can I stay in Vietnam with an eVisa?

Up to 90 days, with single or multiple entry, according to the dates on your eVisa.

How should I write my name on the application?

Use the pinyin spelling exactly as shown in your passport, with the correct order of surname and given names. Do not use Chinese characters.

Yes. We regularly help families and groups prepare their applications together. Send us the number of travellers and your travel dates.
